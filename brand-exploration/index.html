Direction 1 of 3

Calm Retro‑Future

The future as the optimists of the 1970s imagined it, built with today's restraint. Soft pastel daylight, wide unhurried type and objects with generous radii. It feels like a well-made instrument that is on your side.

MoodboardMorning horizon

Observatory dome · concentric calm
Aa Wide, light, unhurried
Perforated aluminium
Soft-cornered controls
unhurried · lucid · optimistic · daylight · well-made Mood words
Frosted acrylic, sea glass

Rationale. Retro-futurism usually goes loud: chrome, neon, synthwave suns. We take the other path, the quiet optimism of mid-century industrial design and early space-age signage, where the future felt clean, legible and kind. That fits a product whose promise is calm.

The rising horizon is the central idea. Work is a horizon you move toward at your own pace, never a wall of overdue items. Pastels carry warmth, and a single deep sea-glass green does the serious work of buttons, focus and links.

Photography and imagery. Daylight, pale skies, architecture with large radii, desk objects with soft forms. People shown resting or thinking, never staring stressed at a screen. No stock "productivity" clichés.

Feels like: a well-made radio, an airport at 6am, a clear schedule.
Never: sci-fi HUDs, neon grids, chrome type, pixel nostalgia.

Direction 2 of 3

Momentum & Continuity

Every piece of work leaves a thread you can pick up again. The identity draws that thread: elegant paths, open orbits and one warm marigold dot that always means "you are here". Progress is shown as distance travelled, never as a gap left to close.

MoodboardLong exposure
Orbital diagram · nothing ever stops, it continues
Light trails
Aa Round, forward-leaning
You are here
Timetables, ticks, relay
continuous · buoyant · directional · resumable · steady Mood words
Rhythm, not streaks

Rationale. The product's hardest job is helping people return after an interruption. This direction makes that the brand. Lines never dead-end, orbits stay open, and a paused task is drawn as a dashed node waiting on the path, not a failure.

Retro-futurism here comes from orbital diagrams, transit timetables and observatory charts. It uses their precision and optimism without their hardware.

Photography and imagery. Long-exposure light, cyclists and swimmers mid-stroke, train windows, hands moving between notebook and keyboard. Motion blur used sparingly.

Feels like: a good transit map, a relay hand-off, a quiet planetarium.
Never: rockets, speed lines, fitness-tracker rings, "crush your goals".

Warm Intelligent Workspace

A desk that tidies itself while you're away. Tactile paper surfaces, soft pressable controls and warm stone neutrals, with a quiet intelligence that notices and suggests but never nags. The future shows up as thoughtfulness, not chrome.

MoodboardThe considered desk
Paper, a bookmark, a good dial
Wool felt, fern
Aa Characterful, kind
Pressable, physical
Bookmarks, ribbons
attentive · tactile · kind · grounded · quietly clever Mood words
Stacked, soft tiles

Rationale. People spend hours a day in this app, so it should feel like a good room. Surfaces have a hint of paper and a gentle lift. Controls look pressable. The assistant speaks in plain sentences and always offers "Leave as is".

The retro-future lives in the details: dial-like timers, ribbon bookmarks and stacked tiles, the warmth of 1960s office design with today's intelligence quietly behind it.

Photography and imagery. Real desks in natural light, wool, ceramic, paper, plants that are actually alive. Hands and objects, rarely faces. Colour-graded slightly warm.

Feels like: a well-kept studio, a librarian who remembers you, a favourite notebook.
Never: skeuomorphic leather, cartoon mascots, sparkle "AI" icons, cosy-to-the-point-of-twee.

Recommendation: Direction 1, Calm Retro-Future

It's the only direction that is calm, warm and unmistakably ours at the same time. The rising horizon expresses "progress without pressure" in a single shape, the pastel-plus-deep-green palette is rare in productivity software, and Lexend's readability makes the system as kind as it looks.

I'd strengthen it with one idea from Direction 2: the dashed "paused node" and the "you are here" marker for the resume experience, drawn in Direction 1's language (soft sun instead of marigold). Direction 3's rule that the assistant always gets its own tint and always offers "Leave as is" should carry over as a product principle whichever route you choose.

Once you approve a direction, the full design system will cover:
  • Final symbol refinement and app icon at every size
  • Colour tokens for light, dark and high contrast
  • Type scale, spacing, radius and elevation tokens
  • Core components: tasks, resume card, focus timer, habits, assistant
  • Motion specs and a reduced-motion variant
  • Illustration kit and photography guidance

Tell me which direction you want, or which pieces to combine, and I'll build from there. The wordmark stays open until the name is final.
